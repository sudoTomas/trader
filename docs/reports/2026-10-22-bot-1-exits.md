# Bot #1's live exits: why they sell the dips

**Date:** 2026-10-22 · **Bot:** #1 (`bot-1`), ETFs SPY/GLD/SLV/USO, live paper ·
**Code:** main `31b81dc` on the spark; the fixes are on branch `worktree-agent-aac0b003264cb26fa`
(`ca2132e`, `bfdaa01`), not deployed.

## Answer

None of the ten ETF closes since 2026-10-01 came from the stop or a breaker. All ten
are model `close` decisions made in the exit-review lane (#34, live on Bot #1 since
2026-09-30) on regular wakeups. Two separate defects drive them:

1. **The lane's wording tilts the decider toward `close`.** The system text says to close
   "beyond the tolerance you stated at entry" (no open rationale ever states one), that a
   hold "must justify staying in", and that "nothing changed" is no justification "when
   the P&L ... says otherwise". The block's ask repeats "prefer `close` ... over `hold`".
   When a long has dipped, price below SMA20/EMA12 and RSI under 50 come almost for
   free, so every close rationale cites exactly those. Four of ten also say the move
   is "beyond (a reasonable) tolerance". The closes landed at a median −0.36%, and 8 of
   the 10 were inside one typical daily move. Re-asking the decider on those same ten
   journaled prompts closes 16 of 20 times with the current wording and 1 of 20 with the
   question asked straight.
2. **Phantom marks from IEX's thin book.** The loop marks equities at the IEX
   latest-quote mid, and IEX often shows one side percent away from the market (GLD
   369.86 x 381.75 when the consolidated quote was 381.64 x 381.67). 211 of 1,061 Bot #1
   ETF wakeups (20%) had a mark more than 0.3% outside that hour's consolidated bar, and
   so did **122 of 239 `price_move` wakeups (51%)**. Three of the ten closes woke on a
   `price_move` with a phantom mark, and two of those had a phantom P&L in the block. GLD
   on 10/16 was closed on a "−2.03%" move while it was really up 2%, and USO on 10/19
   showed −1.92% when it was down 0.17%. A phantom +3.2% move then re-bought USO 30
   minutes later at the same price. Replay never sees this, because replay marks at
   bar closes.

**Does the stop fire too tight?** It never fired. `TRADER_RISK_STOP_LOSS_FRAC` is at
its 8% default (no override in the spark `.env`), and no Bot #1 decision has ever been
journaled with trigger `stop_loss`. Eight percent is about 2.6 typical daily moves for
SLV and 10 to 11 for GLD and SPY. For these ETFs the stop is too loose to matter. The
tight "stop" in practice is the model reacting to the lane's wording.

**Would holding have done better?** About half the time, which makes these exits noise
rather than a skill. Across the ten closes, holding beat selling in 5 of 10 cases four
session hours later (+$34 in total) and in 3 of 8 cases one session later (−$4). The
low-selling is real in the first batch: GLD 10/14 at RSI 19.5 (+1.8% four hours later),
USO 10/16 (+3.6%) and SPY 10/16 at RSI 26 (+1.0%). It is not true of the batch since
10/19: four of those six exits were followed by further falls. The −$50 since 10/19 is
mostly two entries that kept falling (SLV −$33, GLD −$10), which the exits cut short.
Across all 300 held-position wakeups, an oversold reading has mean-reverted on these
ETFs. Holds at RSI < 35 were up four hours later 20 times out of 30 (mean +0.40%), and
holds that were both underwater and at RSI < 45 were up 20 times out of 24. A close in
that state is the sell-the-low pattern. There were only four such closes, too few to
score on their own.

## How the cause was pinned

- **Trigger path:** `decisions.trigger` on each order's decision. None of them is
  `stop_loss`, and no breaker or halt was journaled for Bot #1 since 10/01. Every
  decision's `prompt` carries the "Held position review" block.
- **Phantom marks:** historical IEX and SIP quotes at each mark's instant (Alpaca
  `/v2/stocks/{sym}/quotes`). The journaled mark equals an IEX mid to the cent, and the
  SIP book at the same millisecond is 1–4 bp wide around the fill price. Over a random
  sample of 30 phantom decisions, the IEX quote that produced the mark was found for 28,
  and all 28 had spreads of 70–600 bp.
- **Wording:** a journal re-decide. For each of Bot #1's journaled held-position ETF
  prompts, the harness rebuilds the decide call with the code tree under test (system
  rules plus the block's ask) and asks the decide role again, at temperature 0.2,
  serially, against the same Nemotron the live loop uses. Everything else in the prompt
  (bars, indicators, Macro View, brief, analyst notes, triggers) is the journal's, byte for
  byte. The harness is `~/tmp/trader-exits-scripts/exit_redecide.py` on the spark,
  with `held_study.py`, `trade_table.py` and `phantom_check.py` beside it.

Both halves of the tilt matter: neutralizing either one alone takes the lane from about
8 closes in 10 to 1 or 2. Two variants that pushed the other way were tried and dropped.
They hold every case, including the two exits that turned out right (GLD 10/21, SLV
10/22). The volatility-scaled line showed again the lesson of run 18
(`2026-09-29-exit-review-replay-gate.md`): the model wrote "−1.44% … within the typical
daily move of ~0.8%". A number in the prompt gets used, and used wrongly.

## The fixes

1. **`ca2132e` fix(data): mark equities at the last IEX trade when the quote is wide.**
   This is the deterministic part, with tests in `tests/test_data.py` built from the
   recorded GLD quote. An IEX equity quote wider than 25 bp, or with a side missing,
   does not mark; the last IEX trade does. With no trade, the quote is a data error and
   the existing fallbacks apply (the next provider, then the last bar's close). All 28
   sampled phantom quotes are rejected. On 20 ordinary decisions, 9% of IEX quotes are
   wide enough to take the trade path (one more HTTP call), and the marks they would
   have produced were already right. The new lesson is `docs/lessons.md#iex-wide-quote-phantom-mark`.
   This fix touches no prompt, but it changes which wakeups fire: about half of all ETF
   `price_move` wakeups disappear. It needs a normal deploy and a day of watching the
   journal, and it should not land on the same day as fix 2.
2. **`bfdaa01` fix(exit-review): ask whether the thesis holds without tilting toward
   close.** The ask becomes "State whether the original thesis still holds, and why:
   `close` (full, or partial via a smaller `notional_usd`) when it no longer does,
   `hold` when it does." The system text drops the stated-tolerance clause, "must
   justify" and "nothing changed is not a justification". The test
   `test_the_exit_question_is_asked_without_a_thumb_on_the_scale` locks the tilt out, and
   the golden prompts are updated. This is a decide-behavior change, and the lane is
   live on Bot #1, so **deploying this commit is the flip**. It goes through the gate:
   replay, then a shadow window, then the flip.

What fix 2 costs: on this evidence it takes the lane back to nearly no model exits,
which is close to run 16's "lane off" behavior. That is the honest reading. The decider
shows no exit skill at the hourly horizon on these ETFs. Its closes are a coin flip
against holding, and the right exits it made (GLD 10/21, SLV 10/22) are not
distinguishable in its rationales from the wrong ones (GLD 10/14, USO 10/16). If Bot #1
is meant to exit before an 8% loss, that exit belongs in code, as the 09-30 report
already concluded. One option is a per-symbol or volatility-scaled stop in the #74
stop-loss, which can be backtested on bars with no model calls. Choosing that rule and
its scale is a design decision for the operator. Neither fix here adds it.

The candidate does what the re-decide predicted. It stops the dip exits and the
close-and-rebuy churn, and it still closes SLV at the same step as the baseline, on the
deepest loss with a bearish brief. That is the one exit in this window that a thesis
check should make. In this window it is not a P&L win, roughly −$86 against −$80 once the
replay's unclamped re-open is scaled to the live $1,000. Gold slid 3% over the window,
so the baseline's early GLD exits happened to save money. Exits that pay off only when
the tape keeps falling are the noise this report is about, and they are also why a
deterministic exit, rather than the wording, should be what protects a falling
position. The P&L is mark-to-bars with no costs, as always: it ranks the two wordings,
it does not predict live P&L.

  exercise it (replay marks at bar closes). The evidence for it is the historical-quote
  check above. It is ready to deploy on its own, as its own gate window.
